What Spellbook does well

Spellbook is one of the most recognized legal AI brands for Microsoft Word. It helps attorneys draft, review, and negotiate language across many document types — not only contracts. For firms that want a single AI assistant for emails, memos, and agreements, Spellbook’s breadth is a real advantage.

  • Strong brand recognition in the legal AI market
  • Word-native experience with a mature onboarding motion
  • Broad drafting capabilities beyond contract triage
  • Designed for firms already investing in legal AI platforms

Where Attyflow is different

Attyflow is intentionally narrower. Instead of positioning as a general legal copilot, it focuses on the contract review moments that slow transactional teams down: spotting asymmetric indemnities, uncapped liability, weak confidentiality carveouts, and turning those findings into negotiation-ready redlines.

  • Risk radar first: Each clause gets a risk level, score, and plain-English issue explanation.
  • Redline-ready output: Suggested replacement language you can paste or insert in Word.
  • Position-aware review: Toggle buyer, seller, or mutual perspective before analysis.
  • Lower entry price: Solo plan at $69/month vs typical Spellbook individual pricing.
  • Free SEO tools: NDA, limitation of liability, and indemnification checkers for instant trials.

Workflow comparison

Spellbook workflow

Most Spellbook evaluations start with a demo. Attorneys use the Word add-in to highlight text, ask questions, and generate revisions across a wide range of legal tasks. The platform shines when lawyers want flexible AI assistance without switching applications.

Attyflow workflow

  1. Paste a clause into the sandbox or open the Word taskpane.
  2. Select client position (buyer, seller, or mutual).
  3. Receive risk score, loophole analysis, and proposed redline language.
  4. Accept redline into Word or copy to clipboard for negotiation markup.
  5. Upgrade to Solo or Team when the workflow becomes daily practice.

Pricing and total cost of ownership

Spellbook pricing is typically quoted per attorney and often starts higher for small firms that only need contract review. Attyflow publishes transparent tiers: Sandbox ($0), Solo ($69/mo), Team ($249/mo), and Firm (custom). For a solo transactional attorney reviewing 20–40 contracts per month, Attyflow’s Solo tier is often 2–3× cheaper than platform-style legal AI subscriptions.

Teams should also factor implementation time. Attyflow’s free clause tools let you validate output quality before a sales call — reducing procurement friction for small firms.

Security and trust considerations

Both categories of tools handle sensitive client material. Attyflow publishes a security model, states a no-training-on-your-data policy for submitted contract text, and includes legal disclaimers in-product. Spellbook offers enterprise security options suited to larger firms. If your evaluation checklist includes data retention, subprocessors, and Word-only processing, request each vendor’s security questionnaire before rollout.

Who should choose Spellbook?

  • Mid-size or large firms standardizing one legal AI vendor across practice groups
  • Teams that need drafting help beyond contracts (litigation, correspondence, research-style prompts)
  • Organizations with budget for demo-led enterprise procurement

Who should choose Attyflow?

  • Solo attorneys and boutiques focused on NDAs, MSAs, SaaS agreements, and vendor paper
  • Legal ops teams that need fast clause triage before attorney review
  • Firms comparing Spellbook alternatives on price and focus
  • Buyers who want to test with a real clause before talking to sales

How to evaluate both in one afternoon

  1. Pick one high-risk clause from a recent NDA or MSA (indemnity or liability cap works well).
  2. Run it through Attyflow’s free checker and review the sample output format.
  3. Request a Spellbook demo with the same clause and compare specificity of risk framing.
  4. Score each tool on: accuracy of issue spotting, usefulness of redline language, Word friction, and monthly cost per attorney.
